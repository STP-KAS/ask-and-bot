## Cheap talk and the switch

Cheap talk is the rant, the group chat, the thread, and also a catalog that names Grok, a Roadster, Starlink, or Netflix as if those tills already take a Kaspa stable. It does not bind. The internet already has the rant.

The missing piece is the bound move: you promise a fixed bill; you pick how many others must promise too; nobody sees the running count; if a subset meets every member's number, that subset pays together; if not, your coin never moved. That is the switch. Alone you can still sign. That is not failure. That is the stag.

The vision stays. When a stranger can receive POC-shape or tether-shape money on Kaspa, the same switch points at a real address. Shops that accept those rails are where Kaspa people spend. Until then the examples in [hunts.md](hunts.md) are shapes of a bill, and Hunt Hall is the classroom.

## The game

Two hunters can take a stag together and eat well, or each take a hare alone and eat for a few hours. Once both are on the stag, neither gains by sneaking off. The hard part is the start. If you go for the stag and the other hunter stays with hares, you go hungry. Communities get stuck hunting hares because moving first is the way to lose.

Yonatan calls that stickiness Azazel: people already want to act together, and they have no way to bind the act. Moloch is the other failure, the one where each person gains by cheating. A cheat needs a referee who can punish. A failed start needs a promise that only counts when enough promises count. He calls that promise an intendo. The older name in economics is an assurance contract: I will do this if enough others do it too.

## Stag, pack, hunt

A **stag** is one opportunity. It names the outcome, how the outcome executes, and who may join.

A **pack** is the gathering. People sign intendos. They can leave. While they gather, the running total stays hidden.

A **hunt** is the moment a subset is large enough for every member of that subset. Those members execute together. Everyone else keeps their intendo for a later snap.

One person's threshold can be small. Another's can be large. A tenured professor might move if five colleagues move. An assistant professor might need fifty. One liquidity provider might need a million dollars already in the pool. Another might need ten million. The mechanism has to allow those different floors. It does not pick one cause. The six-pager is explicit that the rails stay open to whatever coordination the users start.

## The four rules

These are the rules he treats as required for the core primitive.

1. **Coordinated atomicity.** The subset whose thresholds are met all execute together. A partial start, or a slow drip of commits, breaks the assurance that nobody moved alone.

2. **Accumulation opacity.** Participants and operators alike cannot see how close the pack is. It crosses, or it does not. Seeing the halfway mark is itself a risk: the first signal can be picked off, front-run, or used to kill a small effort.

3. **Capital multiplexing.** The same dollars can sit in several hunts. A person can back a liquidity move and a lock with the same thousand dollars. Whichever hunt snaps first takes the money. The other intendos remain. This is how one balance can press on many companies at once.

4. **Composability.** The output of one hunt is a valid input to another. Users can switch, and investors can deploy, in one atomic event. A merchant-acceptance hunt can be the condition on a subscription hunt, and that pair can be the condition on the pay of the people who wire it up.

He also wants the snap on infrastructure that is hard to censor, open to anyone, and fair while it is happening. He calls that bundle real-time decentralization. A gap between "the pack is full" and "the money moves" is the place where pressure lands. The village ledger in 1984 has that gap, because the desk's server writes the row. A real hunt settles where the coins live, in the same step.

Two of the four rules, multiplexing and composability, want a shared programmable coin. Isolated payment APIs can take a card payment. They cannot let the same dollars sit, unspent, inside several conditional promises, and they cannot stack those promises into one atomic result.

## What he already priced in dollars

The examples in the six-pager and the Oxford address are dollar amounts.

Liquidity stuck on an old venue: each provider sets a floor, five million, fifty million, five hundred million, and the capital stays productive where it is until a subset's floors are met. The same capital can back exits toward more than one venue. The first exit that fires wins.

A streaming library: viewers commit a monthly fee, in his sketch about five dollars, and the service launches and charges only if enough of them do. Parents ranting on the internet are cheap talk. The intendo is the subscription. Investors can sign a further intendo: I deploy if that viewer hunt completes. Users, capital, and the library arrive together.

Those numbers are dollars. An intendo written in KAS is a different number of dollars every time the quote moves. The hunter who promised "enough for a Starlink month" and the hunter who promised "twenty tKAS" did not promise the same thing. The merchant who prices a car, a share, a song, or a GPU in dollars cannot treat a volatile stack as a cleared pack.

KAS remains the natural collateral under a decentralized stable, and the natural fee, because it is the proof-of-work coin. The unit inside the intendo is the stable. That is why this desk's plan waits on POCencept-shaped money, or tether-shaped money, or both. [rails.md](rails.md) is the classroom. [plan.md](plan.md) is the gate.

## What is an essay

The six-pager is the canon. Fan sites that say live Kaspa already satisfies all four rules overclaim. Toccata, the covenant upgrade, is live on mainnet after DAA score 474165565. A covenant is a rule a coin carries. An intendo stack, a hidden pack, and a threshold snap are a further system. staghunt.ai was a one-word stub when this desk read it. The teaching page shipped inside the review repo is a browser toy on local storage. It takes no wallet. The bot treats a new public artifact from the author as a thing to read, and treats this paragraph as the standing description until that artifact is the snap itself.

Agents make the same game sharper. In the Oxford questions he described agents as clients who need coordination even more than people do, because an agent that moves alone has no salary and no second chance. An agent that pays for compute, data, or a tool needs a budget that is still the same budget when the hunt snaps. A KAS budget is a moving budget. A stable budget is a number the agent and the seller can both read.
