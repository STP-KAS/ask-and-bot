## The idea

1984 puts three ways to pay on one counter so the difference is visible.

**tKAS** is Testnet-10 Kaspa. It is the coin that moves on chain. It is also the fee.

**POCencept** is the picture of a decentralized stable. Prices stay in toy cents while tKAS stays put. Locked toy dollars came from a real tKAS send and can come back. The practice purse spends first and does not come back.

**KUSDT** is the picture of a tether-style stable in the same ledger. It has a freeze. The freeze blocks KUSDT only. POCencept and tKAS still pay.

A shop, a month, a car, a dish, a share, a song, and a model bill are all priced in dollars. KAS moves. A dollar amount written in KAS is a different amount by the time everyone is ready to move. Yonatan's Project Staghunt is a way for people who already want the same thing to move together, once, when enough of them have promised. His own examples are written in dollars: a pool deep enough to enter, a monthly streaming fee, an investor check that fires when the audience does.

So the plan is gated. Staghunt becomes a way to spend when Kaspa has stable money a stranger can receive. That money is a POC-style coin, or a tether-style coin, or both. The company, the dealer, the carrier, the broker, or the catalog that can take the coin the community already holds is where the spend goes. The same dollars can be promised to several of them at once. The first promise that clears takes the dollars.

The village tags are that lesson on a server. They are the classroom. They become the coins in this plan on the day a stranger can receive them under rules the network enforces and the bot can point at the payment.
