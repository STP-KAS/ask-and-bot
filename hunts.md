# Hunts

Each block is a draft the desk can write once [plan.md](plan.md) says the gate is open for that rail. In phase 0 every block is a shape, and the square is the only till.

The brand names below (a lab, a lot, a dish, a catalog) are cheap talk if you read them as live checkout. They are not. They name the *shape* of a bill so Ask does not invent a new category every time. The vision is unchanged: when a stranger can receive POC or a tether-style coin, the community's spend goes to the till that accepts that rail and whose pack clears. Hunt Hall on the square is the switch you can walk today. See [stable-staghunt-theory/IDEA.md](https://github.com/STP-KAS/stable-staghunt-theory/blob/main/IDEA.md).

The same five numbers show up in every block.

- **Stag.** The outcome that is better for everyone who joins, and worse for the person who arrives alone.
- **Rail.** POC when the counterparty can take a coin with no issuer freeze. KUSDT when they need a freeze or a reversal. Both when they can take either, with the two piles kept apart.
- **Intendo.** The sentence a person signs: I pay this many dollars of that coin if at least this many others pay, and if this address can receive the coin.
- **Snap.** The subset executes together. Composed hunts fire in the same step: users, the company, and the people who wire the till.
- **KAS.** KAS is the collateral under the POC shape and the fee on the Kaspa transaction. The number inside the intendo is dollars of the stable, because the bill is priced in dollars.

Multiplexing sits on top of all of them. One pile of POC can be promised to every POC till in the list. One pile of KUSDT can be promised to every KUSDT till. The first hunt that snaps takes that pile. A company that accepts first, and whose pack clears, receives the community's spend. A company that cannot receive the coin has nowhere for the pack to land.

## AI

**Stag.** An AI company takes the stable for tokens, seats, or agent spend. One lab that accepts, with enough committed months, is a place the community can use together. One user with a Kaspa balance and a lab that only takes cards is still hunting a hare.

**Rail.** Both, when the lab can take either. A lab under a freeze order, or a lab that must reverse a stolen key's spend, takes KUSDT. A lab that will custody no issuer key takes POC. Agents are the sharp case: a task that pays for tools mid-flight needs a budget that is still the same budget at the end. Yonatan described agents as clients who need this kind of coordination even more than people do.

**Intendo.** I commit D dollars a month if at least N accounts commit the same month, and the lab's address can receive the coin.

**Snap.** The month starts for that subset together. A composed payroll hunt pays the integration from the same snap, so a builder is paid when the users and the lab actually meet.

**KAS.** GPU time is priced in dollars. A stack of KAS large enough for the month on Monday is a different month on Friday.

**In the square.** The practice purse is the free trial. It spends first. It does not redeem into tKAS. A promotional model credit should obey the same rule.

## Cars

**Stag.** A dealer or a maker takes the stable for a car, a lease, or a fleet. A single buyer who arrives with a coin the lot cannot receive buys nothing. A pack of N cars is an order the lot can tool for.

**Rail.** KUSDT when a lender has to reverse or seize. POC when the buyer pays without handing an issuer that power. Both when the lot's till takes either. The two piles stay apart: a freeze on the loan coin leaves the redeemable coin and leaves KAS.

**Intendo.** I commit the price, or the monthly lease, if at least N vehicles are committed and the lot's address can take the coin.

**Snap.** Titles, or the first lease month, clear together. A fleet for a town is the same hunt with a larger N.

**KAS.** A car is a large dollar price. A move in KAS between the handshake and the title is a different car.

**In the square.** Pike's roadster is 1.00 toy dollar and waits on the lot. The keys are a picture of the purchase. They are a car on the day a dealer can receive the coin.

## Starlink

**Stag.** A carrier or a reseller takes the stable for the dish and for the monthly service. One farm that pays alone is a hare. A town that pays together is a route the reseller can serve.

**Rail.** The bill is dollars every month, so the unit is the stable. KUSDT matches a receivable an issuer may have to freeze. POC matches a town that wants the month redeemable. Both when the reseller takes either. Hardware can be one intendo and the month another, composed so the dish ships when the months are real.

**Intendo.** I commit the hardware once and D dollars a month if at least N households commit, and the reseller's address can receive the coin.

**Snap.** Dishes and the first months settle together. Later months are a fresh hunt, or a standing intendo, still in the stable.

**KAS.** The monthly bill does not change because the coin did. KAS as the unit would make a quiet month and a wild month into different debts.

**In the square.** The launch is a free ride in a toy. The Moon, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn hops are toy-dollar prices. They teach a price list for a trip. They are a Starlink invoice on the day the reseller can receive the coin.

## Stocks

**Stag.** A broker or a venue funds an account, or settles a buy, in the stable. The community's stock buying goes to the venue that can receive the coin and whose pack clears. Other venues keep the card.

**Rail.** KUSDT is the likely first rail, because a broker lives under rules that want a freeze and a reversal. POC is the rail for a venue that will hold a redeemable coin and no issuer key. Both when the venue offers the choice. A buyer can multiplex the same KUSDT pile across every broker who might take it. The first snap wins.

**Intendo.** I commit D dollars to a named basket if the pool reaches T dollars and the venue can receive the coin.

**Snap.** The subset's baskets buy as one atomic order. A composed hunt can fund the account and place the order in the same step, so nobody funds an account that cannot trade.

**KAS.** A share is priced in dollars. Funding the account in KAS stacks the coin's move on top of the share's move. The buyer asked for the share.

**In the square.** Lock is the funding of the account. Redeem is the withdrawal of the backed part. The purse is a bonus that cannot be withdrawn. A broker credit that cannot be withdrawn should be marked the same way.

## Music

**Stag.** A listening service, a label, or a group of artists takes the stable for a month or for a release. Listeners ranting, and artists waiting to see if the listeners pay, is the hare. The stag is a catalog that opens with the audience already paid.

**Rail.** POC fits listeners who want a coin no issuer can freeze. KUSDT fits a catalog company that must reverse a payment. Both when either till works. Artists can sign a composed intendo: the catalog commits if the listener hunt clears.

**Intendo.** I commit D dollars a month if at least N listeners commit, and the receiving address can take the coin.

**Snap.** The month starts for that subset, and the catalog is available in the same step. A release can be its own stag: the record is pressed, or the files unlock, when the pack clears.

**KAS.** A song is a small repeated dollar. Volatility eats the artist's take and makes the listener's month a guess.

**In the square.** Coffee at 2.50 and supper at 14.00 are the small repeated spend. The cent price stays put. A stream should be priced the same way.

## Video

**Stag.** Same shape as music, and a separate hunt, because the rights, the month, and the library are a different pack. This is the content-platform example in the six-pager: a large audience resents the library it has, and a new library never starts, because nobody can see who will actually subscribe. The intendo replaces the rant.

**Rail.** The same split as music. A household can multiplex one pile across several video services and several music services. The first service that can receive the coin, and whose pack clears, takes the month. The others keep the intendo until a later snap or until the person withdraws it.

**Intendo.** I commit D dollars a month if at least N households commit. Investors, in a composed hunt, commit their own amount if that viewer hunt completes. The six-pager's sketch of the viewer fee is about five dollars. The investor sketch is in the millions. Both are dollars of the stable.

**Snap.** Viewers, the library, and the investor capital execute together. A partial launch, viewers charged before the library exists, or a library built for a rant that never pays, is the outcome atomicity is there to prevent.

**KAS.** A monthly video bill is a dollar. The investor's threshold is a dollar threshold. Written in KAS, both move under the hunters while they wait.

**In the square.** A lap of the square is 100.00 toy dollars, a larger ticket than coffee, still in cents. It is the picture of a bigger night out. It is a streaming month on the day the service can receive the coin.

## The wider bills

These use the same five numbers. They are here so Ask does not stop at the six above. The gate in [plan.md](plan.md) still applies to every row. The rail column is the one that fits first. Both remains available whenever the counterparty can take either.

| Spend | Why it is a stag | Rail that fits first | What snaps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Checkout and cards | Ordinary shops never learn Kaspa. A card or invoice rail that spends the stable lets the pack pay them anyway. This hunt composes into every row below. | KUSDT where the card issuer must reverse. POC where the card only passes a redeemable coin. | The card works for the subset on the day the issuer's address can receive the coin. |
| Liquidity | A stable that cannot be entered or exited in size is still a toy dollar. This is Yonatan's first example, and it sits in front of the spend hunts. | POC for collateral that redeems. KUSDT for an issuer pool. | Providers move together when their dollar floors are met. The same capital can back several venues. The first snap wins. |
| Power and heat | A town keeps the old utility because one household cannot make a new one add a till. | Either. The bill is a monthly dollar. | N households and the supplier snap together. |
| Rent | A building fills, or a landlord adds a till, only when enough tenants commit. One tenant offering KAS is a hare. | KUSDT when a deposit must be reversible. POC when the tenant refuses an issuer key on the rent. | Leases start together. |
| Food | The square, grown up. A grocer or a farm box switches in when a neighborhood's weekly spend is committed. | POC. A meal does not need a freeze. KUSDT if the chain requires reversals. | The week's orders clear together. |
| Phones | The general case of Starlink. A plan is a monthly dollar and a handset is a once-off. | Same split as Starlink. | Handsets and the first months snap together. |
| Travel | A flight or a block of rooms happens when the seats fill. Empty seats are the hare. | KUSDT when the ticket must be reversible. POC when it is final. | The party travels, or nobody is charged. |
| Payroll | Builders who wire a till live on monthly dollars. The Kaspero freelancer sheet is the covenant cousin: the network holds the coins, on a schedule, when that sheet is the one in use. | POC. A wage that an issuer can freeze is a different wage. KUSDT only if the employer is under a rule that requires it. | The integration hunt pays when the user hunt and the merchant hunt complete. |
| Compute | GPUs, storage, and bandwidth priced in dollars, distinct from a named chatbot. | Both. The seller's constraint decides. | The rental starts for the subset together. |
| Hardware fleets | Miners, dishes, phones, cars, as a batch. A factory runs when N commit. | POC for a final batch. KUSDT if the batch can be recalled. | The batch is paid and built in one snap. |
| School | A course or a classroom opens when enough fees commit. | POC. Tuition that can be frozen mid-term needs a stated reason, and then it is KUSDT. | The course runs for that subset, or the fees stay put. |
| Health premiums | A mutual forms when enough premiums commit. This row names the category. The policy, the clinic, and the claims desk belong to whoever writes them under their own name. | KUSDT is the usual fit, because a claim reverses a payment. POC if the mutual holds no issuer key. | The mutual starts when N premiums clear. |
| Remittances | A family bill abroad is in dollars. The corridor that delivers a Kaspa stable takes the send. | Both, on purpose. Some corridors require a freeze. Some families cannot accept one. | The send arrives in the coin the family chose. |
| Public goods | I fund this if others fund it. The six-pager keeps the rails unopinionated. This repository does not pick the cause. | POC. A public good an issuer can freeze has a censor. | The good is funded by the subset, together, or the pledges stay in the pack. |
| Tickets and games | A night, a match, or a server opens if enough seats commit. | POC for a final ticket. KUSDT for a refundable one. | The event happens for that subset. |
| Attention | An advertiser commits a budget if an audience pack forms. The six-pager says a hidden pack still has to meet attention, or the effort stays a dark forest. The publisher who can be paid in the stable gets the spend and the audience. | The stable the advertiser and the publisher both accept. | The budget and the audience snap together. |

## How a draft is written

When the gate for a rail is open and the desk asks for a draft, Ask writes one page with the five numbers, the receiving address once it exists, the dollar amount, the threshold, and the composed hunts. The bot checks that the address can receive that coin and that the master file agrees the coin is spendable. The draft stays in this repository. Posting it is a separate ask.
