## Say this first when someone wants Staghunt for a real bill

The square can be walked today on Testnet 10. tKAS is the coin that moves. POCencept and KUSDT are tags in the village ledger. Prices in the shops are toy cents, so a coffee stays a coffee while KAS moves.

Project Staghunt is Yonatan's way for people who already want the same thing to move together: each person promises to pay if enough others pay, the total stays hidden, the same dollars can sit in several promises, and a fitting subset all pays at once.

A real bill is priced in dollars. His own examples are dollars. An intendo written in KAS changes size while the pack waits. So a hunt for AI, cars, Starlink, stocks, music, video, or any other bill in [hunts.md](hunts.md) waits until a stranger can receive stable money on Kaspa. That money is a POC-style coin, or a tether-style coin, or both. The village tags do not open that gate. The master file is the place that records whether a spendable layer-1 stable exists. While it says none does, you describe the hunt as a draft.

## How the money works

Four jobs, split across three names.

tKAS moves on Testnet 10. It is the collateral you lock, the coin a redeem returns, and the fee. Every send pays twice the node's ordinary fee, and never under 200 sompi per gram. The fee is extra. It is outside the price.

POCencept is the picture of a decentralized stable. Locked cents came from a tKAS send and can redeem. The practice purse is 20.00, spends first in a shop, and does not redeem. There is no freeze. If the outside KAS quote moves, a redeem can fail because the lock no longer covers the cents. That is a peg failing.

KUSDT is the picture of a tether-style stable. Same lock, purse, and redeem. Its booth can freeze it. Frozen KUSDT does not pay. POCencept and tKAS still pay.

At a quote of $0.05 per KAS, one toy dollar is 20 tKAS. The page uses the live quote. Shop prices stay in cents either way. Coffee is 2.50, supper is 14.00, the roadster is 1.00, a lap is 100.00.

A shop buy and a toy swap ask on the page. The wallet signs only a tKAS bank swap, and only for the address already on the page. The shop card is the village ledger. The tKAS bank line is a Testnet-10 transaction.

## How a hunt works, once the gate is open

Pick the rail from the counterparty.

- They can take a coin no issuer can freeze: POC.
- They need a freeze or a reversal: KUSDT.
- They can take either: both. Keep the piles apart. A freeze of KUSDT leaves POC and leaves KAS.

Write five lines.

1. Stag: the outcome that is better together and worse alone.
2. Rail: POC, KUSDT, or both, and why.
3. Intendo: I pay D dollars of that coin if at least N others do, and if this address can receive it.
4. Snap: the subset pays together. Stack a merchant hunt and a payroll hunt on the same snap when the till still has to be built.
5. KAS: collateral under POC, and the fee. The unit inside the promise is the stable.

Then say the multiplexing rule in one sentence: the same pile can be promised to every counterparty in that category who might accept, and the first snap takes it. That is why the community's spend lands on whoever can receive the coin. It lands when their pack clears. It is an incentive in the mechanism.

Use [hunts.md](hunts.md) for AI, cars, Starlink, stocks, music, video, and for power, rent, food, phones, travel, payroll, compute, hardware, school, premiums, remittances, public goods, tickets, attention, checkout, and liquidity. Liquidity sits in front: a stable nobody can exit in size is still a toy. Checkout sits underneath: a card that spends the stable lets the pack pay shops that never add a Kaspa till.
